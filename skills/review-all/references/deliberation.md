# Deliberation — from committed report to filed program

Phase 6 of `/review-all`. The run ends at the report; this protocol turns its structural findings into decisions, a program doc and `/prd` sessions, and closes with the contract §11 trigger chain. It is human-attended throughout; never run it under AFK autonomy. `/review-all --yes` (SKILL.md §Parse invocation) changes one thing here: an item whose analysis converges on a recommendation is ruled by the lead without waiting, and the ruling says so. It does not remove the human from an item that does not converge, from an "accept as standing" disposition, or from committing the trigger-chain gate.

## What goes to the human

Everything structural (contract §6) is deliberated; mechanical findings were already filed by the run. In this first sitting each candidate stream plays the part a plan plays in a wave's sitting, and its questions are its open choices and the items below. Order the streams by the synthesis matrix, contested items first:

1. a stream with `open_choices`;
2. a contradiction the synthesis could not resolve;
3. an invariant candidate;
4. a `weakened` finding the matrix still ranks in a cell's top three;
5. a critic join error the lead overruled, and any `critic_disputes_severity` the digest flagged;
6. any stream sized `L` or risk `high`;
7. any stream whose `deletable` is empty (a remedy that adds without removing is a cost increase until shown otherwise).

Uncontested streams (one option, confirmed findings, size S/M, low risk) are questions too, each with its one recommended option; the pre-filled answer sheet makes accepting them a matter of leaving their lines alone.

Every sitting, this first one and each wave's author-pass sitting (§Launching step 2a), is one written sitting document, prepared as §Preparing a sitting says, in the form §The sitting document fixes, and answered in chat. Measured on the first dark-factory program: the human stopped ruling a 60-question list written in drafting shorthand, unable to read its labels; the same questions rewritten in this form were ruled in one pass with 11 discussion points, and the next wave's 32 questions and four carried-over points in one reply.

## Preparing a sitting

In this order; the human sees nothing until step 6 reports the page ready.

1. **Triage every open question.** One seat sorts every open question of the round (the authors', the critic blockers that need a ruling, the points carried over from earlier rounds) into exactly one class, reading the plans, the program doc §Resolved decisions, and the code or the store where a class turns on a fact:
   - *human*: it changes what the running system does (behaviour, failure handling, defaults, thresholds, what operators or agents see), reverses or narrows one of the human's rulings, cancels or re-scopes queued work of real value, changes a public contract or a board figure, spends or saves significant compute or merge-queue time, or has options between which a reasonable human could choose on taste or strategy;
   - *lead*: names, file homes, seam ids, task order, task splits and sizes, which stream owns an artefact both would build, test strategy, the wording of a ruling whose intent is unambiguous;
   - *duplicate*: the same decision asked in two plans is asked once, in the plan that owns the artefact, and not at all when a recorded ruling settles it (cite the ruling).

   When unsure, it is the human's. A question the author marked `needs_human` stays the human's unless it is shown to be a duplicate; the author's `lead_rulable` and `needs_human` flags are inputs to the triage, never its verdict. Two plans answering incompatibly, or one symbol given two homes, get one ruling that binds both. The seat drafts the lead's ruling for every lead and duplicate point; the lead adopts or amends the draft.
2. **The lead rules its points and lists none of them.** Each is recorded in the program doc §Resolved decisions with `ruled_by: lead` (§Recording). No list of minor points is written for the human, and the human is never asked to confirm one: it goes unread. A lead point that changes running behaviour, or follows the intent rather than the words of one of the human's rulings, is not buried either: it becomes a question for the human, or one line under the question it belongs to (§The sitting document, *Decided by the lead under this question*).
3. **One writer seat per plan** turns that plan's questions for the human into one structured section with the fields §The sitting document lists. It reads the plan draft, the return's `open_questions` (the source of truth for which questions exist, their options in order, and the recommendation), the stream's background and the human's earlier ruling on it, the critique for facts, the lead's rulings, and the live code and task store read-only. It keeps the source's options and recommended option; a doubt goes in the doubt field, and only the lead changes a recommendation (§The sitting document, *Facts*).
4. **Mechanical check** of every section by script, fixed until it passes: the question ids are exactly the triage's set for the human for that plan; option count and order match the source; the recommended option is the source's, or carries the lead's change; every required field is filled; each issue opens with "The choice:"; and a scan finds none of the shorthand the plain-language rule forbids.
5. **Render by script** into one document, the points carried over from earlier rounds as its Part 0, with an answer key beside it (document key → plan, source question id, option labels) so the reply is read mechanically.
6. **Cold read.** A fresh seat that sees only the rendered page reads it in order, as the human will, and lists where the human could not decide or would be misled: a name or number with no explanation or base; an unverified consequence of the recommended option; a dependency on another question's answer left unstated; a recommendation whose reason applies equally to an alternative; a doubt that proposes an answer the sheet does not offer; a behaviour change outside any question; counts, marks or letters that disagree between the introduction, contents, quick view, body and answer sheet. Its fixes go back to the writer seats (only what the cold read names, every fact a fix needs checked), then the document is re-rendered and read cold again, until the cold reader reports it ready. Measured on the first dark-factory program's third wave: two cold reads; the first found 1 blocker (the recommended option's only stated consequence was unverified) and 17 majors.

Push back on an unstated assumption in a question's framing. Do not relitigate a ruling recorded in a previous program doc unless the finding's evidence changed; cite the ruling.

## The sitting document

It is written for the human: someone who knows the system's architecture, has read none of the report, plans, critiques or briefs, and rules from the page alone. A vague explanation becomes a wrong ruling that agents then build, so accuracy comes before brevity and plain words before completeness of detail. It lives in the program's state home.

**Plain language.** None of these appears unexplained: a stream number (say what the stream does; the number may follow in parentheses); a task label such as a Greek letter (say "the task that …"); a plan-internal id for a contract, decision, boundary row or question; anything from the drafting process (author, critic, fold, review-round ids), which is not mentioned at all unless a fact from it matters, and then the fact is stated; a finding key; a ruling id (give its date and quote or paraphrase the sentence that matters); a bare task number (say what the task is and its state, the number in parentheses); an invariant id (say what it requires); a heuristic number (name the heuristic); an abbreviation coined in a plan. A code name is fine when it is the thing being decided, with a few words on what it does at first use. Sentences are short, one idea each, in the active voice, with no em-dashes, arrows or semicolons. Every consequence comes from the plan, the critique, the code or the store, with its number when a source has one and "not measured" when none does, and is stated in what the human weighs: correctness and silent failure, what the next agent must know, operator burden and escalations, merge-queue and CPU cost, which queued tasks are cancelled, delayed or made obsolete, and whether one of the human's rulings is reversed.

**Parts, in order.**

1. A title naming the round, and one line saying it is written for a reader who has not seen the plans and that statuses were re-checked on the day it was written.
2. *What this is*: where the program stands (what the review found, what was ruled when, what this document covers) and its counts: questions, how many marked higher stakes, carried-over points.
3. *Words used throughout*: a table of every term that recurs across plans, the marks included, and one line naming each plan mentioned in passing by what it does.
4. *How to answer*: the keys (`<plan>-<n>` for a question, a separate prefix for a carried-over point), the option letters, and the replies accepted: accept all; accept all except a list of key and letter; an addition or a question on any key, which opens a discussion of that point; a request for a separate investigation. A question left unlisted is accepted as recommended. The answer sheet is at the end.
5. *Contents*: one row per plan with its question and higher-stakes counts, linked.
6. *Quick view*: per plan, one line per question (key, linked; the question; the recommended letter and option name; its marks), so the human can accept in bulk and open only what matters to them. Carried-over points get a table of their own.
7. *Part 0, carried over*: the points from earlier rounds still awaiting the human, each in the anatomy below. One already answered elsewhere says so, quotes the reply and how it was read, and stays on the sheet pre-filled with that answer.
8. *Part 1, the questions by plan*: per plan a summary (what it works on; the problem today, with one or two concrete examples; what the plan changes, in outcome terms; what the human already ruled; size and risk; the draft's path, for a reader who wants it), then a table of the names its questions use, then its questions.
9. *Answer sheet*: one fenced block, a line per key pre-filled with the recommended letter, the question as a comment, and its marks. Nothing else: no line for any point the lead ruled.

**One question**, the same anatomy every time so rulings are comparable:

- *Heading*: the key and the question in plain words, at most about fifteen, ending in "?", with the higher-stakes mark when it applies. Headings carry the keys, so a viewer's outline navigates the document.
- *Situation changed* (only when it applies): a banner saying the live system moved since the question was drafted in a way that affects the answer, and that the text describes it as it stands now.
- *The issue*: opens with "The choice:" and one sentence naming the decision, then the situation that forces it (what exists today, what the plan wants to do, what collides or is undecided), concretely: the behaviour, the numbers, the tasks described. Findings are described in words, with their measurements and rules, how far the review stands behind them (a weakened finding says so), and any severity the critic disputed.
- *Why it matters*: what goes wrong, or is wasted, if this is decided badly; for a finding, the next change an agent makes here and what it costs today.
- *Options*, lettered in the source's order, one per source option: what it means (what would be built or done); its effect on the issue (fully, partly or not at all, and what remains); its other consequences (on other work, operators, running behaviour, queued tasks, and the long term: what the next change costs a year on, what becomes deletable, which invariant is enforced or weakened); its cost and risk. "Accept as standing" is an option when the cost is real but contained; it files an `accepted:<task>` disposition with a reason, never silence. An answer the analysis or the cold read recommends is a lettered option, never only a suggestion in the doubt.
- *Recommendation*: exactly one, unmistakable, in its own block (never on the line after an indented list, where markdown folds it into the last item), with a reason that separates it from the closest alternative. A question whose analysis does not converge goes to an investigating seat before it is put; if it still does not converge, the block says so and why.
- *If you disagree*: the most defensible alternative, and when it would be the better call.
- *A doubt worth knowing about* (optional): only where the live re-check gives the human a reason to choose otherwise.
- *Invariant candidate* (when present): what it would require, its checkable form and fixture sketch in plain words, and the invariant it extends. Promotion is in checkable form only, via the four-site lockstep edit `CONTRIBUTING.md` §6 describes (quality doc §Relationship to the design invariants).
- *Decided by the lead under this question* (when present): one line per lead point that belongs here and changes running behaviour or reads one of the human's rulings by its intent (§Preparing a sitting step 2).
- *Live status, checked the day it is asked*: for every task the question or its options name, its status and one-line meaning read from the store; whether the code the question rests on has changed on main, with the commit checked. "Nothing this question depends on has changed" only after checking.
- *To answer*: the line that accepts the recommendation.

A skeleton, nothing more:

```
### <plan>-<n>. <the question in plain words>?  <higher-stakes mark>
**The issue.** The choice: <one sentence>. <what exists, what the plan wants, what collides>
**Why it matters.** <…>
**Option A: <name>** RECOMMENDED · what it means · effect on the issue · other consequences · cost and risk
**Option B: <name>** · <the same four>
> **Recommendation: Option A (<name>).** <the reason that separates A from B>
**If you disagree.** <…>   **Live status, checked <date>.** <…>   To answer: `<plan>-<n>: A`
```

**Higher stakes.** The mark picks out the questions that cost most to get wrong: one that reverses one of the human's rulings in substance, cancels queued work of real value, or is hard to undo once built. Every question in the document changes something the human weighs, or the triage would have given it to the lead, so the definition in *Words used throughout* states the rule exactly as applied, and the counts in the introduction, contents, quick view and answer sheet agree. Measured: a first cold read found six unmarked questions that changed live behaviour under an introduction that said the mark meant exactly that.

**Facts.** Every fact a question rests on is checked before the document is posted (each named task with `get_task`, each code claim at main); a question the human must decide never carries an unchecked fact. A recommendation the measurements contradict is changed by the lead, and its block says it was changed and why; a writer seat's own doubt goes in the doubt field. Measured on the first dark-factory program: one wave-2 recommendation flipped because a task it assumed pending had started; a wave-3 recommended option's only stated consequence held only once another task's renderer was read.

Under `--yes`, the sitting document is still written, every question with its recommendation; the lead then records each recommended option as the ruling without waiting. A question with no recommendation, an "accept as standing" option the lead would pick, or an invariant promotion stops for the human exactly as without the flag.

## Reading the answers

The human answers in chat, by key and letter, and lists only exceptions, additions and questions:

- A question the reply does not list is accepted as recommended and recorded `ruled_by: human`; the lead's reply names those keys back.
- An addition to a ruling ("B, plus …") is part of the ruling: it is recorded with it, and the plan changes and follow-up tasks it creates go into the ruling fold.
- A question back, a request to discuss, or "discuss with <key>" opens a discussion round before the lead replies. One investigating seat per plan (the plan's writer seat, resumed, holds the context) measures against the live code and task store and writes, for each ruled point, the ruling as understood with every addition, whether it is buildable with the facts checked, its exact effect on the plan, any consequence the human may not have seen, and a direct answer to any question inside it; for each open point, a short answer, the facts with how each was checked, the options now including the human's own proposal (said plainly when it beats the recommendation), a proposed answer, and its effect on the plan. Points the human links are investigated together.
- A request for a separate investigation is launched as its own session (the `/team` escalation path, §Launching), and its ruling returns into the program.

The lead then processes the rulings and brings back only the questions the answers leave genuinely open or newly raise and that the human must decide, in a residual-questions document prepared and written as above; never a list of what the lead ruled. The round ends when no residual question remains.

## Recording

Each ruling is recorded as soon as it is read, before any discussion round reports:

- in the program doc §Resolved decisions: the ruling with every addition the human made, the options considered, the consequence that decided it, the keys it covers, and `ruled_by: human`, `ruled_by: lead` (a point the triage gave the lead) or `ruled_by: lead (--yes)`;
- as one `decisions_and_rationale` memory with `entities=` naming the tasks or program once they exist (`project_id`, `agent_id` per the overlay), carrying the same `ruled_by` in its text; the program doc is the home, the memory is the pointer;
- an "accept as standing" ruling files a task carrying `x_finding_key`, `x_finding_run`, `source="review-all"` and then cancels it with `metadata.x_acceptance_reason = "<the ruling>"` (contract §7–§8); a `deferred` task is postponed work, never an acceptance, and a `cancelled` task without the reason reads as abandoned. The next run's step 1b keys on that field. `--yes` never makes this ruling.
- a reversed or reworded ruling is a NEW ruling carrying `supersedes: <the earlier ruling>`, never an edit of the old one (solar_challenge_platform R15 was reworded in place; reify S22 R1's reversal lived only in a to-do file);
- a ruling the human makes inside a spawned session comes back in its return's `rulings_in_session` (§Launching) and is recorded here like any other, `ruled_by: human`.

## The program doc

Written once the agenda is exhausted, at the path `report-format.md` §Artefact 3 names, with every section it lists. Streams are sized for one `/prd` session each, and every stream authors in `agent` mode: a stream with open design questions is not a different mode, its questions go to the wave's author-pass sitting (§Launching). Each stream lists its finding keys; the G4 table gives every shared artefact one owner before any PRD is authored. G4 is seeded from outside the program too: before authoring, and again at each wave boundary, scan non-terminal PRDs' Files lists and tasks' `metadata.files` outside the program for each stream's touched paths and add their owners as G4 rows (measured on reify: the `codim` clash with pre-existing #6669 surfaced only after S9 had filed). The doc is committed with its wave (§Commit cadence).

## Launching `/prd` per stream

The lead sequences authoring and decomposition; seats and spawned sessions never commit (§Commit cadence) and never edit a shared document. This is what the first dark-factory run found it needed: seats depended on each other's filed task ids for seam dependencies, and six concurrent `git commit` calls in the machine-operated checkout would have contended on the index lock.

- **The seat brief** names the program doc, the stream row, the finding keys and the freshness clause, carries the report's `## Mechanical filings` rows for the stream's keys **verbatim** (never the lead's summary of them — the lead's briefs twice asserted a Phase-5 task that did not exist), and instructs the seat to re-run contract §8 step 1 immediately before filing regardless of what the table says.
- **Seat returns** (author, decompose and investigate): ONE structured JSON block, schema `review-all-prd-return/1` — top-level `open_questions` (each with `needs_human`), `rulings_in_session`, `xrefs`, `lead_actions`, and on the decompose pass also `filed`, `held`, `released`, `moves` (`op` ∈ `cancel` | `edge` | `note` | `stamp` | `repath` | `release` | `amend`); field shapes and the `moves` list (paths and symbols the stream moves, renames or deletes, consumed by the delivered-check scan below) per `plans/review-all-program-tooling-prd.md` §Return block (committed with this amendment) — written to the program's state home at `~/.claude/review-all/<run_id>/inbox/<S>.<pass>.json` (the one channel; a spawned session's `result.md` names that path and carries no second copy). The lead applies it, by hand until the program-tooling PRD lands.
- **Per-stream passes**: one `/team` run, one seat per stream, `opus`/`high`, in two lead-sequenced passes, author and decompose, with the critic between them:
  1. **Author.** Each seat runs `/prd` author against the program doc with the overlay's conventions and writes its PRD, capability manifest and sidecar to disk; it returns its block and does not commit. Its open questions are not answered in the seat and not ruled by the lead on sight: they wait for the author-pass sitting (step 2a). The PRDs go into the wave's one commit (§Commit cadence).
  2. **Critic and fold** (between the author pass and the wave commit). An adversarial critic — `opus`/`xhigh`, or `fable`/`high` for a stream sized `L` or risk `high` — is handed the PRD, manifest and sidecar and the stream's open questions with the author's recommendations, and attacks premises, buildability, invariant enforcement, semantics, seams and manifest polarity → fold (the critique applied on disk, by the author seat while it is live, else a fresh `opus`/`high` seat; the fold seat re-emits the return with the critic blockers that need a ruling added to `open_questions` as `source: critic`). The author-pass sitting (2a) follows the fold; the fold-verifier (`opus`/`high`) → second fold run as agent seats once the rulings exist. The fold-verifier is mandatory for size `L` or risk `high`; for other streams a lead spot-check may replace it; a purely prose or check-re-anchoring stream needs neither. Measured on reify: every critic that counted found ≥ 1 blocker (self-reported); two refuted lead rulings made without a code check (S4 OQ-1: the seal created a module cycle; OQ-3: a donor a single-spelling grep missed); fold-verifiers found new majors on every stream that counted (S1 2 blockers, S12 9 majors). Price these seats at the cost gate (SKILL.md §Cost gate) under one stated token-counting rule — reify's 0.3–0.7M per critic and 0.25–0.6M per fold-verifier were under an unstated one.
  2a. **The author-pass sitting** (one per wave, after every stream's critic and fold, before the fold-verifier and the commit). Every open question of the wave — the authors' plus the critic blockers that need a ruling — is triaged, and those for the human go into one sitting document for the whole wave (§Preparing a sitting, §The sitting document); the lead rules the rest and lists none of them. The answers are read and discussed per §Reading the answers, and the human may escalate an item to a spawned `/team` investigation (the escalation path below) whose ruling returns into the program and closes the item. Once no residual question remains, the fold-verifier and decompose run as agent seats, and the lead makes the wave commit; a `needs_human` question that surfaces at decompose goes into the next residual-questions document. Measured: 8 of 47 spawned sessions carried design discussion; all 11 reify free-text overrides answered questions that already had a recommended option; spawn-to-pass-1 median 21.7 h.
  3. **Decompose.** The lead orders the streams by the seam graph — a stream that others depend on first (here S14 → S18/S21 → S25 → S23) — and hands each seat, in that order, the filed ids it depends on plus the sitting's rulings on its open questions. The seat decomposes under `planning_mode`, adds its `high`/`critical` leaves as dependencies of the trigger-chain gate, runs `commit_planning` on its own batch, writes the stamped sidecar to disk, and returns its block without committing. Three gotchas of the step: (a) a `cancel` move whose step-1 target is in progress with a live heartbeat becomes an `edge` instead — the new leaf waits on the running task, which is never cancelled out from under its agent; (b) a parent is cancelled only after its surviving children are folded into stream leaves, never before (scp left five orphaned L1s, and Leo's standing rule of 2026-10-17 requires the order); (c) a planning-mode `submit_task` that times out is not retried — the call may have created the task, so read the store first — and `commit_planning` stamps one sidecar per call, so a batch committed in several calls leaves several sidecars to reconcile. A leaf that waits on a later stream is a **held leaf** (`report-format.md` §Artefact 3 defines `x_seam_wait`, `release_owner` and `x_seam_released`): filed, wired, excluded from `commit_planning`. Its release owner — the owning stream, or the lead when `release_owner` says so — releases it in four writes, plus a fifth on a re-stamped hold: (1) `add_dependency` onto the delivered leaf; (2) the description rewritten to the contract as delivered; (3) `x_seam_released` stamped; (4) `commit_planning` on that leaf, which runs the delivered-check polarity gate and, once that passes, stamps the sidecar whether or not it flips the row; (5) only if (4) returns `not_planning_hold` (the leaf carries the `until_condition` hold from `report-format.md` §Artefact 3), `set_task_status(status="pending")` on that leaf. `set_task_status` never comes first: it runs no polarity gate, and the leaf is schedulable the moment it is `pending`. Recipe: `plans/review-all-program-tooling-prd.md` §H4 `release`.
  4. **Delivered checks.** (a) Each decompose return lists the paths and symbols the stream moves, renames or deletes; the lead's clerk pass scans every non-terminal task's `metadata.delivered_checks` for those paths and emits the corrections in the same batch, each as three writes: the capability-manifest yaml, its md twin, and the task's `metadata.delivered_checks` as the COMPLETE list. (b) Check patterns are single-line and symbol-anchored; a multi-word phrase that can wrap across comment lines is rejected (reify S23: 9 such checks, 6 vacuous at authoring). The polarity gate's two-sided measurement — fail at the authoring tree, pass after the producer lands (`shared/src/shared/delivered_check_polarity.py`) — is unchanged. (c) Each check is named substrate (verdict `PASS`, green by design) or producer, so the right polarity rule applies. Measured: reify ran six correction rounds over ≈ 50 checks on ≈ 40 tasks in ≥ 10 PRDs; solar_challenge_platform met three hazard shapes (over-broad `absent`, sibling-green, moved spelling).
  5. The lead verifies each filed batch with `get_task` (not `search_tasks`), applies the returned blocks and cross-reference lines to the program doc, and puts the stamped sidecars and the FILED rows into the wave's one commit (§Commit cadence).
- **The `/team` escalation path** (not a stream mode — no stream is a `spawn` stream): when the human escalates one item of a sitting (step 2a, §Reading the answers), the lead spawns one interactive session for that item via `skills/spawn/SKILL.md`, with a brief file under the overlay's brief directory in the shape above. The hotspot program (`plans/bug-hotspot-remediation-program-2026-07-06.md`) is the proven shape. The spawned session investigates the one item and returns its block to the lead rather than editing the program doc; its ruling returns into the program and is recorded like any other (§Recording). The brief tells the session to checkpoint `result.md` after each pass — a checkpoint carries `outcome: in-progress`, only the final write a terminal outcome (the vocabulary is `skills/spawn/SKILL.md` §Result-handback's; `in-progress` is this program's convention until the program-tooling PRD adds it there) — to write `HANDOFF.md` on a context or usage limit, to run `/reflect` before ending, and to END after returning its ruling. Self-termination arrives with the program-tooling PRD's opt-in Stop-hook self-exit (σ2); until that lands the session idles after its ruling, so the lead watches `result.md`, not the process: an interactive spawned session waits by design (measured: both Phase 2 sibling sessions of two runs idled ~5 h after finishing).
- Every `/prd` session stamps `metadata.x_finding_key` (list) and `metadata.x_finding_run = <run_id>` on each task it files and runs the contract §8 dedup before filing; the FILED section is written by the lead from the rows the sessions return.

## Commit cadence

All docs changes of a wave — PRDs, manifests, sidecars, the program doc, the state snapshot — go through the lead (or its clerk) and are committed ONCE per wave, in one `git commit --only <paths>`. Seats and spawned sessions never commit; reify's sessions committing their own PRDs is retired. Every docs-direct commit advances main and tends to force the in-flight merge's re-verify; queues are usually busy, and around the dark-factory and reify runs nothing merged for ~10 h; more than `MAX_CAS_RETRIES` (5) direct commits in one merge flight blocks that merge (dark-factory `CLAUDE.md` §Working in the main checkout). The rule holds until the docs-only merge gate (dark-factory task 5411; a 30-day shadow is ruled) lands; after that, program docs go through the merge queue as each PRD is ready.

## Closing: the trigger chain

The contract §11 chain is the program's last act, filed exactly as `skills/_shared/filing-the-trigger-chain.md` §§0–5 says, with `skill = "review-all"` (the bare skill name, per its §0) and this run's `run_id`; nothing about its shape is restated here. What is specific to this skill:

- **Timing.** The gate is filed (§4 interim, or §3 final when the §2 probe in the dark-factory checkout passes) when the first `/prd` stream files; every later `/prd` session adds its own `high`/`critical` tasks as dependencies as it files; `commit_planning` happens only once the program doc's FILED table is complete. `--yes` never runs that `commit_planning`: the human does.
- **Supersede first.** An older open `review-all` chain (§1) is superseded per §5 before this run's gate is filed. When this run was launched from that chain's `review_due` escalation — the normal path once `plans/completion-driven-triggers-prd.md` lands, its id having arrived as `--launched-from` (SKILL.md Phase 0 step 5) — §5 step 2 is where it is resolved, with `ran as <run_id>`.
- **No tasks filed** (program filed nothing): no chain, and the report's `## Method` `extra` says so (§0).

Record the gate id(s) in the program doc's FILED section, then write the run's `observations_and_summaries` memory with `entities=` naming the gate task(s). If the store is unreachable, file nothing and say so in the program doc; the next session files the chain.
