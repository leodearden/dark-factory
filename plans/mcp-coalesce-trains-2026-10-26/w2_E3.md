# E3
Post-4953 train failures are overwhelmingly a member-quality and plumbing problem, not a cross-member or MCP problem. Of the 11 'Train union verify failed' derails, none shows a genuine cross-member interaction and none is a ratchet-ledger conflict between members. Eight have at least one member whose own branch trips the failing gate (reproduced alone in a scratch clone, or failed the same test as a solo verify; one of the eight, 5445, also sat on a red base), and three are flake/infra-class (6588, 6201, 6413). The 3 rebase derails are member-vs-main conflicts (2 confirmed from rerere specimens, 1 undetermined), so a repaired member-vs-member line-range screen would have kept apart 0 of 3. The 10 dissolved/lost trains were all caught in the buffer by a fleet drain. Every train whose buffer interval contained a drain request (10 of 56 ended trains) was dissolved. Dissolution wastes no verify compute, but its 26 members take a median 11.6 h to land afterwards, of which 8.5 h is the flip-to-pending re-dispatch. Waste over the window: derailed trains burned 10.83 verify host-hours (10.84 h by my independent sum) of 225.7 h total. About 5.9 h of that is own-defect members, 2.6 h is a red-main train, 1.8 h is flakes, and 0.6 h is 5445. The levers are therefore: screen members for own defects before admission, order verify legs so cheap whole-tree legs fail first, stop starting trains on a base the lane just judged red, and re-queue dissolved members directly. Even all of them together are worth only about 3 to 5% of verify compute. For the MCP question, the finding is a precondition: MCP singles (27 of 82 are resubmits of a branch last blocked, per E1) probably carry a higher own-defect rate, and an own-defect member poisons the whole train.

## FINDINGS
- No post-4953 union-verify derail shows a genuine cross-member interaction or a ledger conflict between members. The 'Train union verify failed (re-verifying members as singles)' tag over-reports interaction.
  - evidence: Of 11 trains, 8 have a member that fails the cited gate alone (7071: 5563; 3678: tip 3678; 5445: 5172; 5957: 5957 and 7096; 6856; 5737: 7462; 6738: 4701; 6387: tip 6387), 3 are flake/infra-class. The members that overlapped on a baseline file (5172/5445 on merge_lane_ratchet_baseline.json, 7421/6387 on gates.py and the baseline) already failed individually. 5445 is tagged 'union' although its codebook failures reproduce on the bare base. E2's count of '8 of 11 name ratchet or baseline-style tests' is correct about the tests but not about the cause.
- The dominant derail cause is a member's own defect that the package-scoped workflow verify cannot see and that is first detected at the whole-tree merge gate, typically in the scripts/ leg (inline-suppression ratchet, drift guards, ratchet baselines).
  - evidence: inline_suppression_ratchet failed the merge gate for 34 distinct tasks in 12 days, with solo and train tips alike. 5563 adds unowned 'type: ignore' lines in a new test file and fails `inline_suppressions.py --check` alone with excess 7. Tip 3678, member 4701 and member 5172 do the same. The scripts leg runs about 4 min but ends 6 to 7 min before the end of a 35 to 49 min verify, so a train spends its whole verify to learn something a 3-second script already knew.
- Two of three rebase-conflict derails are a member conflicting with current main (the first stacked member), not a member-vs-member conflict; a working get_changed_line_ranges screen would not have prevented any of the three, and it is broken anyway.
  - evidence: Specimens MERGE_RR.5179-20261024T131654 (reconciliation/harness.py) and MERGE_RR.5453-20261024T134345 (agents/roles.py) match scratch rebases of the first members 5912 and 5192 onto the train base; member pairs 5912/5179 and 5726/3211 share no files. 3155 and 5453 do share the baseline json, but it is not the conflicting file. orchestrator/src/orchestrator/merge_lane/worker.py::SpeculativeMergeWorker._stack_coalesce_train calls GitOps.get_changed_line_ranges(task_id) with the bare task id and git_ops.py builds `git diff main...{ref}`; I re-read both in the worktree. These derails cost no verify, but 9.8 buffer-hours for 7 members and a one-strike mark for each.
- Every post-4953 train caught in the lane buffer by a fleet drain was dissolved; dissolution wastes no verify compute but delays members by about 11.6 h, mostly because members are flipped to pending and re-dispatched by the scheduler.
  - evidence: 10 of 10 trains whose buffer interval contained a drain request were dissolved or lost, and 0 survived; 6 of 32 drain requests hit a buffered train; the 10-22 08:00Z drain removed four trains at once and orphaned a fifth (7258). Read in worktree: worker.py::SpeculativeMergeWorker._dissolve_coalesce_train sends members to MemberRedriver.redrive, journald logs 'flipped to pending for solo-merge re-dispatch', and its docstring states nothing journals a train or its absorbed singles. Measured dissolution-to-requeue median 8.5 h, queue-to-done median 152 min.
- The lane started a train on a base it had just judged red.
  - evidence: 6057 was judged 'Main-health red: reproduces on bare main' for the codebook tests at 08:57:23Z on 10-24; 5445 started at 08:58:27Z on base 0f443e6c, where the same two codebook tests fail with no member merged, and spent 38 min before being tagged 'union verify failed'. The 'not present on main' baseline used for that tag was therefore stale or absent.
- Rewriting what a 'train derail' costs: derailed trains also keep innocent members out of trains and out of landing for days.
  - evidence: 17 of the 29 members of the 11 union-derailed trains are still pending 1 to 9 days later with updated_at equal to the derail time (never re-dispatched). Seven of 15 post-4953 one-strike exclusion rows concern innocent members (7071, 6703, 7394, 5726). Members that did land took 1.5 h to 50 h after the derail.
- For the MCP question, the evidence cuts toward needing a member-quality gate first: the failure rate per train rises with member count, and MCP singles are the population most likely to carry an own defect.
  - evidence: 8 of 46 started trains (17%) carried at least one own-defect member, at roughly 10 defective members among 111 members in 46 started trains; E1 reports 27 of 82 MCP singles are resubmits of a branch whose last outcome was blocked (not independently re-measured here). A defective member takes the whole train down, as 7071 (3 members), 6856 (3) and 5737 (3) show: each had two clean members that would have merged. I did not measure the MCP defect rate directly; this is an inference.
- The verify legs after the first failing leg still run, and the cheap legs run last.
  - evidence: For 6413 and 5737 the tests_scripts leg ran after the scripts leg failed; for 6856 the orchestrator leg ran after fused-memory failed. Scripts-leg failures end +23 to +43 min into 31 to 49 min verifies. Reordering to scripts-first with fail-fast would have ended 7 derailed trains in about 5 min instead of 31 to 154 min, about 6.25 host-hours, but fail-fast could interact with the 'new failing tests not present on main' comparison, which needs a complete failing set; I did not check that code path.

## CAVEATS
- Reproductions are approximations. I merged member branches (git merge) into the train base instead of replaying the lane's rebase, used current task/<id> branch tips (pending tasks are unchanged since the derail; done tasks were reconstructed from merge_sha^2, the landed tip rebased on a later main), and for done members could not reproduce the original defect (6856, 5737, 6413, 6588). For those the evidence is lane records (solo failure of the same test, then pass) rather than my reproduction. The 6856 union could not be built because the reconstructed landed tips conflict.
- Several class labels rest on the flake ledger's 'fails_in_isolation' verdict, which for the b10, task_verify_dispatch and history_leg tests contradicts my clean isolated reruns. I classed those as flake/infra because they fail at many unrelated tasks and pass on rerun, but I cannot prove nondeterminism. 6413 is genuinely unresolved (class e).
- The 5445 base-red finding is verified at base 0f443e6c; I did not identify the commit that broke the codebook tests, so I make no claim about which direct-to-main commit caused it.
- The Q3 dissolve-to-landing latencies mix the dissolution effect with each task's own re-run time and with the 10-22 and 10-26 restarts and a scheduler pause (10-26 05:42-12:37Z); 5 of 26 members have not landed yet. The 5991 members (158 to 170 h) are outliers. 'Dissolve-to-landing minus a train-counterfactual' is not computed because no like-for-like landing time exists.
- Pre-4953 rebase derails (8) and the 3211 conflict file are not reconstructed. Journald starts 2026-10-07 and keeps only some rerere specimens. A broad `journalctl --grep` over several days times out under load, so I used narrow windows around drain requests; the full-journal 'dissolving it' grep did complete and returned exactly 13 lines.
- Journal timestamps are local time (BST, UTC+1) until 2026-10-25 02:00 and UTC after; I converted them. events.timestamp is UTC. The window is trains formed from 2026-10-13T21:48Z to now (57 formed, 56 ended, 46 started), one more than the lead's data cut at 10-26T16:28Z, so some counts differ by one from E2's.
- All lever estimates are upper bounds assuming perfect detection and zero screening cost, using the lead's 16 min per member saving and E2's 0.67 start-to-merge rate. They are not additive (L1 and L2 overlap). Savings of 6 to 8 h are 3 to 4% of the 225.7 h verify compute in the window.
- I did not read the verify.py or runner code for a fail-fast option or for how the 'new failing tests not present on main' baseline is built; both statements about them are inferred from log timing and from the derail text.
- Scratch files are in /tmp/e3 (probe.py, probe.sh, stack.py, q1c.py, q3b.py, legs.py, waste.py, bufwait.py, onestrike.py, two shared clones). No repository file was modified, no stash was used, and no task or escalation write tool was called.
