# E1

Measured from runs.db (read-only, events since 2026-07-01, last event 2026-10-26T16:28Z). Discriminator: a merge_queued event with no open workflow merge phase for (run, task) is MCP-origin; journal-recovery re-queues are traced back to their original submission by request-id reuse. It labels all 21 known MCP tasks correctly (21/21). Since 2026-08-01 there were 454 MCP-origin submissions against 2,500 workflow ones, and 295 of 1,914 landings (15.4%) were MCP. These used about 17% of verify host-hours (217.8 of 1,283.4 h). Since 4953 (2026-10-13T21:48Z): 82 MCP singles; 13 distinct request ids were excluded as no_awaiting_workflow, only 8 of them MCP (the other 5 are journal-recovered workflow requests). At most 13 of the 82 MCP singles had an eligible partner waiting at the same time and were themselves train-eligible (4 of those 13 also appear in an exclusion event; 9 left no event). A merged train verify costs far less than the solos it replaces: on the local host a size-2 train takes about 51 min against about 78 min for two solos, and a size-3 train about 46 min against about 117 min. The sub-figures behind these are in `figures`; the limits are in `caveats`.

## FINDINGS
- [read] The only discriminator code-reachable in runs.db is indirect: neither merge_queued nor merge_finalized records who submitted. merge_queued carries only branch, queue_depth, and optionally reason/position/source. No merge_queued row carries source (0 of 5,806 since 2026-04-24), so the stranded-reaper tag is never present in the event store.
  - evidence: orchestrator/src/orchestrator/merge_lane/worker.py::_emit_merge_queued and ::enqueue_merge_request; sqlite: select json_extract(data,'$.source'),count(*) from events where event_type='merge_queued' group by 1 gives a single NULL group of 5,806. The escalation MCP merge_request path emits no merge event itself (escalation/src/escalation/server.py::merge_request emits only workflow_verify, and only when verified_green).
- [read] Reliable MCP marker: an enqueue with no open workflow merge phase. Workflow submits only from inside a merge phase, so phase_enter(merge) precedes the merge_queued by seconds (e.g. 7484: phase_enter 15:44:51, queued 15:45:06). MCP submissions have no phase_enter for the task in that run (6669 queued 2026-10-23T08:36Z with none; 4743, 5811 likewise).
  - evidence: orchestrator/src/orchestrator/workflow.py::TaskWorkflow._submit_to_merge_queue (sets awaited_by_workflow=True; submit inside the merge phase). runs.db sequences for tasks 6669, 7484, 6725, 7421, 4743, 5811. Validation: 21/21 known MCP tasks labelled MCP at their landing request.
- [read] Journal recovery is the main false-positive source for the NO_PHASE test and must be traced through request-id reuse: a restart finalizes in-flight requests as 'blocked: Merge worker shutting down', and the recovered request re-queues in the new run with the SAME request_id and no phase_enter. 363 of 981 NO_PHASE queued events since 07-01 were such re-queues. Recovery rebuilds the request without awaited_by_workflow, so recovered workflow requests are also excluded from trains.
  - evidence: 6669: mr-3176dc73 finalized 'Merge worker shutting down' at 2026-10-22T08:23:20Z in run-51c8eb8e65fc, re-queued 08:23:46Z in run-657feb3e8c0f with no phase_enter, finalized again at 10:25Z. orchestrator/src/orchestrator/merge_lane/queue_store.py::reconstruct_merge_request builds MergeRequest(...) without awaited_by_workflow (default False in merge_lane/types.py::MergeRequest). 5 of the 13 post-4953 no_awaiting_workflow exclusions are workflow tasks re-queued by recovery (5263, 5463, 5868, 6586, 7335 at 2026-10-17T05:41Z).
- [read+measured] The premise holds but MCP is not dominant. About 15% of landings and about 17% of merge-verify host-hours since 2026-08-01 are MCP-origin (295 of 1,914 landings; 217.8 of 1,283.4 verify rows-hours). Since 4953 it is 57 solo landings, 33.2 h of 219.5 h (15.1%). Weekly share is typically 9-20% with a W35 outlier of 58%.
  - evidence: Q2 and Q5 figures. Verify wall times fell sharply since the laptop runner arrived: MCP solo passing-verify median is 13.5 min on laptop against 38.2 min local, so a solo MCP verify now costs about 14-40 min of host time.
- [measured] The counterfactual population is small. After the train gate, 82 MCP singles were queued in 13 days; only 13 had both an eligible workflow-awaited partner waiting at the same time and a clean own-branch history. Only 4 of those are visible in exclusion events; 9 are invisible (suppressed). Most MCP requests either wait <1 s in the buffer (48 of 82) or are resubmissions of a branch whose last outcome was blocked (27 of 82), which the default recent_terminal_blocked predicate would exclude regardless of the awaited flag.
  - evidence: Q3b reconstruction. Code: orchestrator/src/orchestrator/merge_lane/worker.py::SpeculativeMergeWorker._default_coalesce_exclusion_reason (one-strike and recent_terminal_blocked/error), ::_maybe_coalesce_waiting_singles (needs >=2 eligible candidates in the normal-lane buffer, then _select_train_members and a max of merge_train_max_members=3).
- [read] The pre-4953 train incidents were real but not universal: of 54 trains with an MCP-origin member, 14 merged and 33 hit the member-deferred pre-check. Even so, MCP members DID land in merged trains before 4953 (16 task landings, including carriers 5540 5926 6017 6065 6377), because the status check sometimes saw merge-deferred; the rule 'a train lands only if every member is merge-deferred' is a probabilistic gate for MCP members, not an absolute one.
  - evidence: Q-extra figure; train_member_deferred payloads such as 'status is pending, expected merge-deferred' (task 5766, 2026-10-12T15:33Z); worker.py::_do_train_merge step (a) status_check via harness.py::build_train_callback_factory (read in the brief, not re-opened in this seat).
- [measured] The verify saving per absorbed single is large when a train lands: size-2 trains cost 34-45% less host time than two solos, size-3 about 60% less (naive, local mean 46.4 min against 3 x 39.0). Offsetting cost: since 08-01 only 88 of 210 formed trains merged and 51 derailed, with 133 train verify rows (104.7 h) of which 34 failed.
  - evidence: Q4 train figure. Derail reasons are recorded in train_derailed.data.derail_reason (e.g. coalesce-6387-30499418: 'Train union verify failed (re-verifying members as singles)').
- [inferred] The 2026-10-14..16 gap in train_coalesced events (none) and the 4953 deploy time are not separable from events; the only observed post-gate evidence starts 2026-10-17T07:02Z.
  - evidence: train_coalesced counts by day (10-13: 2, 10-14 to 10-16: 0, 10-17: 5). Deploy time not read.

## CAVEATS
- Precision of the MCP discriminator is NOT established: there is no ground-truth list of workflow landings and the MCP server leaves no event. Only recall (21/21 on the known set) and proxies (phase_exit cross-check 99.6%, tip_sha-less workflow_verify marker) were measured. The label 'MCP' means 'no workflow merge phase at origin' and therefore also contains any non-workflow submitter (stranded reaper, other hand paths), which cannot be told apart because merge_queued never records data.source.
- In-phase MCP submissions are mislabelled as workflow: an interactive merge_request issued while a workflow's merge phase is still open (e.g. 6669's second queue at 2026-10-22T05:44Z) looks identical to a workflow resubmit. Upper bound from the marker: 56 of 2,500 workflow-labelled origins (2.2%) carry a tip_sha-less workflow_verify within 48 h.
- MCP calls that return already_merged at the door, 'attached' to an in-flight entry, or are door-coalesced produce no merge_queued row, so true MCP submissions are undercounted by an unmeasured amount. merge_coalesced events stop after 2026-09-18 in this store, so they cannot fill the gap.
- Pairing merge_queued to merge_finalized is by task and run (merge_queued has no request_id). About 0.8% of events (25 of 3,273) overlap another open request for the same task; 7 of those mix NO_PHASE and workflow. A tie-break was applied; the earlier untuned pairing mislabelled task 7189.
- A crash that left no merge_finalized row is recovered only heuristically (earlier-run unmatched queued event for the same task). 53 queued events since 07-01 never finalize.
- Q3b is an upper bound and is not a measurement of what the coalescer would have done. Not visible in events and not modelled: the lane field (high-lane MCP requests are never coalesced; lane is not in any event), line-range stackability (_select_train_members) and rebase conflicts in stack_train_branches, max 3 members, the _last_coalesce_signature debounce, and that the pass runs only at merger-loop tops with can_coalesce() true. merge_dequeued.queue_depth is only the raw asyncio queue size, not lane-buffer depth, so occupancy comes from queued/dequeued/finalized intervals only. Phantom rows were not investigated.
- The 4953 window start (2026-10-13T21:48Z, merge commit time) is not the deploy time. No train formed between then and 2026-10-17T07:02Z, so the earlier part of the window has no counterfactual evidence either way.
- Week W35 (2026-08-24..30) shows 124 MCP-labelled submissions (58% of landings): concentrated 08-27..08-31 with many 'blocked' and 'Merge worker shutting down' outcomes. Its cause (hand-carry during an incident) is inferred, not read; the discriminator could also be mislabelling workflow requests there, since the cross-check was not run per week.
- merge_verify.duration_ms is runner dispatch wall time. Verify-hours here are host-hours summed over parallel hosts (local + laptop), not queue-slot wall hours, and are not CPU-hours. Remote vs local runners differ about 3x (about 14 min vs about 38-41 min), so means depend on the runner mix. The train-vs-solo saving compares different task populations and ignores derailed or failed train cost.
- The 5 train-absorbed known carriers (5540 5926 6017 6065 6377) landed through trains, so the brief's list item 'MCP landings' includes train landings; only 16 of the 21 have a solo 'done' row. Landings count only merge_finalized 'done' rows and train_merged member tasks; 'already_merged' (42) and other terminal states are excluded.
- Not established: how much of the verify saving survives derails (re-verify as singles cost not separated), per-train marginal cost by member, and any statistic after the DB's last event (2026-10-26T16:28Z). run_id boundaries (about 8 h per run) were respected for pairing and verify-row attribution but chain linking across runs depends on request-id reuse.
- Three load-bearing code claims were re-opened and confirmed in the worktree: (1) worker.py::SpeculativeMergeWorker._coalesce_exclusion_reason returns COALESCE_EXCLUDED_NO_AWAITING_WORKFLOW when not candidate.awaited_by_workflow, before the predicate; (2) workflow.py sets awaited_by_workflow=True at MergeRequest construction in the submit path, while escalation/server.py::merge_request builds MergeRequest without it and emits workflow_verify (no tip_sha) only under 'if verified_green and event_store is not None', and queue_store.py::reconstruct_merge_request omits it; (3) _maybe_coalesce_waiting_singles requires req.lane == 'normal' and len(candidates) >= 2 (and again >=2 after exclusions). Analysis scripts are scratch files in /tmp/e1 (final.py is the consolidated one); the DB was opened read-only and no repo file was modified.
