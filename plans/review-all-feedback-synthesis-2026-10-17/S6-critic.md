## 5. Verdict

The draft's structure is sound and most of its Tier A is well-evidenced, but it should not go to Leo as written. Four items are wrong in a way that would cost him: B3 sends him to restart a service that already serves the tool; A1(b) presents a store read that reify forbids and omits the scripted MCP route Phase 5 already uses; B4 recommends a cadence that the cited ruling's own paragraph says can block a merge, on evidence from commits that were not the program's; and A6.2 would have the `/prd` overlay accept exactly the checks `commit_planning` rejects. Below those, the §1 "measured facts" over-read S4's inferences (the context dichotomy, the seat-cost figures, the findings-per-token constant), A3 prices itself across two token rules, A4 retires the blind-deliberation option while claiming to preserve a choice, A5.1 answers a drift signal with five re-marks, and A7 under-sizes the second sitting. Three agenda items (1-timing, 6, 10) are resolvable from the sources and should be dropped or reduced; the retro critic on reify's wave 1 and a human-hours calibration are the two missing items most worth adding. Nothing in the draft reverses a failure-bought rule without answering the failure — "seats never commit" and "one editor" are correctly left open with reify's counter-practice named — and no Tier A item is already landed except the parts of A4 and A8 noted above.
